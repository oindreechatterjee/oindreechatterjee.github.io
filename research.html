SIGCSE TS 2027 · First author

AutoRemind, a Reminder System for Managing Course Deadlines

Oindree Chatterjee, Aaryan Mehta, Zixuan Zhuang, Irene Chung, Kevin Chen, Jacob Shaul, Lance Santana, Dan Garcia

Students often struggle to keep track of course deadlines and requirements across the various platforms modern courses rely on, which can increase stress and lead to missed assignments. We present pilot results from AutoRemind, a personalized notification system that consolidates deadline reminders and relevant course resources into a single channel of the student’s choosing (email, text, or Discord). In an 8-week CS1 summer course, 170 students were randomly assigned to receive AutoRemind reminders or to a control condition; 114 completed both pre- and post-intervention surveys. Mental effort spent tracking deadlines decreased among students who received reminders and increased among control students, a significant between-group difference (p = 0.003). Intervention students also showed a numerically larger increase in confidence managing deadlines, though this difference was not significant. Over 60% of intervention students said they would use the system again. We describe two new features designed to improve the system’s scalability and usability: a self-service instructor portal and a calendar integration. We also outline a larger fall pilot (approximately 1,500 students across three courses) to test these findings at scale with behavioral outcome measures.

Are CS0 Grades Less Consistent Predictors of CS1 Outcomes After Mastery-Based Assessment?

Kerry Pan, Andrew Phuong, Oindree Chatterjee, Jamil Shirinov, Jadyn Miyahira, Maya Sriram, Dan Garcia

CS0 courses provide a foundation for success in CS1 and are often used to signal readiness for subsequent coursework. At the same time, many institutions have adopted mastery-based assessment approaches that emphasize reassessment, feedback, and flexible pacing. Because these systems allow multiple opportunities to demonstrate proficiency, they may change how grades are interpreted. This raises a key question: has mastery learning changed the value of an A?

This paper examines how the transition to mastery-informed assessment in a CS0 course relates to downstream outcomes in CS1, which remained comparatively stable over the study period. Using data from multiple offerings before and after the transition, we analyze how the relationship between CS0 performance and CS1 outcomes evolves.

We find that CS0 grades remain positively associated with CS1 outcomes after mastery-informed assessment, though downstream outcomes become more variable for students with the same CS0 grade. Estimated correlations are directionally smaller after mastery. However, we do not find definitive evidence that mastery-informed assessment degrades the value of an A.

The Cost of Catching Up: Investigating the Impact of Late Enrollment on Student Success in a CS0 Course

Victoria Phelps, Sahana Bharadwaj, Aananya Lakhani, Zihao Huang, Heidy Hernandez, Oindree Chatterjee, Jordan Schwartz, Stacey Yoo, Dan Garcia

Late enrollment in computer science courses presents unique challenges that may impact student performance, engagement, and retention. This paper investigates the academic trajectories of late enrollees compared to their on-time peers, focusing on performance across different assessment types, the cumulative impact of coursework, and engagement with course resources such as office hours, discussion sections, and review sessions. Using enrollment data, course performance metrics, and student engagement records, we conduct an analysis of academic outcomes across various demographics and examine potential policy and instructional interventions, such as modular course structures or expanded support services, to mitigate performance gaps. Our findings contribute to ongoing discussions on equitable access to computer science education by identifying structural barriers that hinder late enrollees' success.

Too Late to Succeed? Demographic Trends and Academic Performance in Introductory Computing

Victoria Phelps, Sahana Bharadwaj, Zihao Huang, Aananya Lakhani, Oindree Chatterjee, Heidy Hernandez, Jordan Schwartz, Anneliese Galler, Stacey Yoo

Late enrollment in introductory computing is common yet consequential. Analyzing two offerings of an introductory computing course (Term 1: n=204; Term 2: n=79), we find sharply elevated failure rates for Week 4 enrollees (Term 1: 41% F; Term 2: 100% F) versus on-time peers (Week 0 failures: 10% and 2%). These outcomes coincide with markedly lower engagement in support resources (office hours, exam reviews). While some subgroups (e.g., URM late enrollees) show pockets of resilience, performance by gender experiences dramatic changes. Results suggest late entry beyond Week 2 in cumulative, fast-paced courses is a high-risk policy point, and that structured catch-up and mandatory early support are critical.

Developing and Sustaining Summer Bridge Programs

Oindree Chatterjee, Carolyn Wang, Alvaro Monge, Colleen M. Lewis, Narges Norouzi

We aim to bring together colleagues who share a common interest in Computer Science and Engineering Bridge programs, which are designed to bridge the gap for less experienced and/or underserved students as they begin their university studies. We invite those interested in starting bridge programs at their institutions, as well as those knowledgeable about existing bridge programs, their best practices, and lessons learned. Together, we discuss the most effective approaches to these programs and challenges that arise in their implementations, including exclusivity in the selection of admits, target audience, funding limitations, outreach, and more. Our goal is to foster conversation and create a network of support so these programs can sustain despite general discouragement.

SIGCSE 2025 · Poster · First author

AutoRemind: Improving Student Academic Performance Through a Personalized and Automated Notification System

Oindree Chatterjee, Ankita Sundar, Arihant Choudhary, Connor Robert Bernard, Dan Garcia

University students, with their busy schedules and endless assignments, often struggle to remember due dates, upcoming tasks, and available course resources. We are developing AutoRemind, an opt-in notification tool that delivers personalized reminders for upcoming tasks and course resources via students' preferred communication platforms. AutoRemind integrates with Learning Management Systems (LMS) to extract real-time data on student progress, assignment status, and available resources. This microservice, built for the GradeView platform, serves as a centralized hub for students to access all course deadlines and resources from a single source of truth. The tool is not specific to computer science classes, so it potentially has very broad reach, hopefully leading to improved academic performance and increased course engagement. We aim to reduce administrative burdens on instructors and support mastery learning, enabling students to progress at their own pace while maintaining consistent academic performance.
